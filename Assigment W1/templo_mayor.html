Templo Mayor Museum: The Heart of the Aztec Empire

The Museo del Templo Mayor is an archaeological site and museum located in the Historic Center of Mexico City. Opened in 1987, the museum was built to house and protect the incredible discoveries made after the accidental finding of the Coyolxauhqui stone monolith in 1978, which led to the excavation of the main religious center of the Mexica (Aztec) people.

In ancient Tenochtitlan, the Templo Mayor was considered the center of the universe. It was a massive twin-pyramid dedicated to two main deities: Huitzilopochtli, the god of war and the sun, and Tlaloc, the god of rain and agriculture. Today, visitors can walk along elevated pathways directly over the exposed ruins of the different construction stages of the temple.

Inside the museum, eight exhibition halls display more than 7,000 artifacts recovered from the site. Highlights include monumental stone sculptures, offerings of shells and coral from distant coasts, ceramic vessels, and obsidian blades. A visit to the Templo Mayor offers a unique look into the religion, daily life, and impressive engineering of the Aztec civilization before the arrival of the Spanish in 1521.
