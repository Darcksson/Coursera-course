National History Museum: Inside Chapultepec Castle

The Museo Nacional de Historia is located inside Chapultepec Castle, sitting high above Chapultepec Park with panoramic views of Mexico City. Originally built in the late 18th century as a summer residence for the Spanish viceroys, the building has served many roles throughout history, including a military academy, the imperial palace of Maximilian I and Carlota, and the official presidential residence until 1939.

In 1944, the castle officially opened its doors as the National Museum of History. Its twelve exhibition rooms guide visitors chronologically through the major chapters of Mexican history, starting with the fall of Tenochtitlan and the colonial period, moving through the War of Independence and the Reform era, and ending with the Mexican Revolution of 1910.

Alongside its historical galleries, the museum allows visitors to explore the preserved living quarters of the castle, known as the Alcázar. Here, you can see 19th-century European furniture, lavish gardens, stained-glass galleries, and famous murals painted by renowned Mexican artists like José Clemente Orozco, David Alfaro Siqueiros, and Juan O'Gorman.
